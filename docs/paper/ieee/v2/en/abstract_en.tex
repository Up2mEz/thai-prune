Open vision-language models (VLMs) for Thai OCR now read most of a page correctly, but Thai vowels and tone marks are small, and one mark can change a word. This paper asks where the marks are lost, in addition to how many. We introduce a mark score that ignores reading order, penalizes surplus text, and gives no credit to the copies produced by a repetition loop. On the ThaiOCRBench calibration split, Typhoon OCR 1.5 (2B) writes a wrong tone mark in only 0.65\% of the places where it reads the base consonant correctly. Given the correct preceding text, it chooses the right tone mark 98.8\% of the time even without the image. In six open models, most of the marks are lost because text is absent from the output, not because marks are misread: absent text costs 7 to 12 times more marks. Among training-free decoders, stopping at a detected loop raises mark F1 for every model, by 0.8 points for Typhoon and by 10.8 to 24.4 points for models that loop often, and it changes almost no output that ended on its own. A repetition penalty lowers F1 on pages that do not loop, and subtracting the language prior removes correct tone marks. \todo{The image test for other models and a locked-split confirmation are pending.} All results are preliminary. They come from one benchmark and a calibration split, and some rules were written after the data had been seen.
