Open vision-language models (VLMs) for Thai OCR now read most of a page correctly, but Thai vowels and tone marks are small, and one mark can change a word. This paper asks whether these marks are the weak point of open Thai OCR models, and where they are lost. We introduce a mark score that ignores reading order, penalizes surplus text, and gives no credit to the copies produced by a repetition loop. We develop the analysis on a calibration split of ThaiOCRBench and confirm it on a locked split of 352 items, read once, with eight open models. The answer is mostly no: in all eight models, text that is absent from the output costs 4.7 to 15.8 times more marks than a mark misread after a correctly read consonant. Given the correct preceding text, Typhoon OCR 1.5 (2B) and Pathumma choose the right tone mark about 99\% of the time, with little help from the image. Qwen3-VL-4B knows the marks as well but misreads some of them when it writes the page itself, so the finding holds only in part for general models. Stopping at a detected repetition loop raised mark F1 in 12 of the 13 model-and-task cells where a model loops on about 5\% of pages or more, by 4.8 to 35.6 points, and it changed 1 of 2,400 outputs that had ended on their own. The one cell that did not pass, Pathumma on full pages, rarely loops. A repetition penalty lowers F1 on pages that do not loop, and subtracting the language prior removes correct tone marks. All results come from one benchmark. Typhoon comes from the group that released it, and Pathumma's training data are not named, so we do not rank the two models.
