Open vision-language models (VLMs) for Thai OCR now read most of a page correctly, yet one question keeps its practical weight: Thai vowel and tone marks are small, and one mark changes a word. This paper asks where the marks are lost, not only how many. We introduce a mark score that does not charge reading order, charges surplus text, and gives no credit to the copies of a repetition loop. On the ThaiOCRBench calibration split, Typhoon OCR 1.5 (2B) writes a wrong tone mark in only 0.65\% of the places where it reads the base consonant correctly, and, given the correct preceding text, it chooses the tone mark correctly 98.8\% of the time even without the image. The marks it loses are mostly never read: lines are absent, spans are missing, or the output is consumed by a loop. Among training-free decoders, stopping at a detected loop leaves every non-looping output unchanged and raises mark F1 by 0.8 points for Typhoon and 10.8 points for a 0.9B Thai model; a repetition penalty lowers F1 on pages that do not loop, and subtracting the language prior removes correct tone marks. \todo{Other models and a locked-split confirmation are pending.} All results are preliminary: one benchmark, a calibration split, and rules written after the data were seen.
