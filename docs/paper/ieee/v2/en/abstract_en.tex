Thai vowels and tone marks are small, and one mark can change a word. We ask whether these marks are the weak point of open Thai OCR models, and where they are lost. We introduce a mark score that ignores reading order, penalizes surplus text and gives no credit to copies produced by a repetition loop. We develop the analysis on a calibration split of ThaiOCRBench and test it once on a locked split of 352 items with eight open models. Most lost marks belong to text that is absent from the output: in all eight models, absent text costs 4.7 to 15.8 times more marks than a mark misread after a correctly read consonant, and 2.0 to 3.6 times more if every misread mark is counted. On the calibration split, given the correct preceding text, the two Thai fine-tunes, Typhoon OCR 1.5 and Pathumma, choose the right tone mark about 99\% of the time, while Qwen3-VL-4B misreads some marks as it writes. On the locked split, cutting each output at a detected repetition loop, done offline, raised the mark F1 estimate in all 13 registered model-and-task cells and passed the registered test in 12 of them (gains of 4.8 to 35.6 points), and changed 1 of 2,400 outputs that had ended on their own. The registered criterion that every eligible cell pass was not met, because Pathumma on full pages did not pass. All results come from one benchmark, and we do not rank Typhoon against Pathumma.
