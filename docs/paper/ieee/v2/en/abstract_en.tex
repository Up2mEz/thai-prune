Thai vowels and tone marks are small, and one mark can change a word. We ask whether these marks are the weak point of open Thai OCR models, and where they are lost. We introduce a mark score that ignores reading order, penalizes surplus text and gives no credit to copies produced by a repetition loop. We develop the analysis on a calibration split of ThaiOCRBench and confirm it once on a locked split of 352 items with eight open models. The answer is mostly no: in all eight models, text absent from the output costs 4.7 to 15.8 times more marks than a mark misread after a correctly read consonant. Given the correct preceding text, the two Thai fine-tunes, Typhoon OCR 1.5 and Pathumma, choose the right tone mark about 99\% of the time, while Qwen3-VL-4B misreads some marks as it writes. Stopping at a detected loop raised mark F1 in 12 of the 13 registered cells, by 4.8 to 35.6 points, and changed 1 of 2,400 outputs that had ended on their own. All results come from one benchmark, and we do not rank Typhoon against Pathumma.
